Taniti Travel Guide

Guide

Getting Around

Visitors can explore Taniti using several transportation options. Public buses serve Taniti City, while private buses provide transportation throughout the rest of the island. Taxis are available in Taniti City, and rental cars can be obtained from an agency near the airport. Bicycles and helmets are also available for rent from several vendors.

Taniti City is relatively flat and easy to explore on foot. Merriton Landing is also designed for pedestrian access. Visitors should be aware that public buses operate from 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. daily.

Frequently Asked Questions

What currency is used?

Taniti uses the U.S. dollar, although many businesses also accept euros and yen.

What language is spoken?

Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English, while English is less commonly spoken in rural areas.

What electrical outlets are used?

Taniti uses 120-volt electrical outlets, the same voltage used in the United States.

Know Before You Go!

Health & Safety

Taniti has one hospital and several clinics. The hospital has many multilingual employees. Violent crime is rare, but visitors should remain aware of petty theft and pickpocketing, particularly as tourism increases.

Alcohol

The legal drinking age on Taniti is 18. Alcohol cannot be served or sold between midnight and 9 a.m.

Travel Planning

Visitors should plan transportation and accommodations before arriving. Taniti offers a range of lodging options and transportation services for exploring the island.

Local Customs

Taniti has a rich local culture, with native architecture visible throughout Taniti City. Visitors can explore the island's history through its local history museum and experience its growing tourism community.

Taniti celebrates several national holidays, which may affect business operating hours. Visitors should plan ahead when traveling during holidays.
